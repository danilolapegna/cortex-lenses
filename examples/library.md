# A library people can navigate

Illustrative example. No reader trial has been run.

**Problem.** A volunteer library has fixed shelves, two volunteers and no budget.
Readers struggle to locate a suitable title. Any improvement must preserve inventory
accuracy and add no weekly work.

**Direct answer.** Put a clear subject map at the entrance and consistent labels on
shelves. Keep existing shelf IDs so volunteers do not have to maintain a second inventory.

> **Index by questions, not by shelves** · *lens: change representation*
>
> **Before:** an item is found through its location. **After:** through the questions readers actually ask, each pointing to the same shelf ID.
>
> **Kept intact:** inventory accuracy, existing shelving, no extra weekly work.
>
> **New operation it enables:** answering “something for a ten-year-old who loved comics” without knowing the collection.
>
> **Cheapest test:** ten real reader questions; compare time to the right shelf using the current map and a question index tied to stable shelf IDs.
>
> **Drop it if:** the index needs weekly upkeep.

**Decisive dependency.** Reader questions must map to persistent subject categories
without promising that a particular title is in stock.

**Breaking point.** If common questions require title-level availability updates,
the index becomes a second catalogue and adds weekly work.

**The proof to seek.** Alternate the two aids across comparable reader tasks, record
time and wrong turns, and inspect whether the question index points to stable shelf
categories or needs title-level updates. Keep it only if retrieval improves without
extra upkeep. Faster retrieval is still a hypothesis; this example reports no result.

## After an explicit choice: an illustrative continuation

Suppose the user says, “Explore the question index and the existing-duty-slot
pilot.” This selection and the objections below are invented to show the checks
in action. No selection, permission, observation or reader trial actually occurred.

For **the question index**, *Fragile Assumption* exposes a gap: questions about a
specific comic can require live stock information. The proposal narrows to
category-level questions, with title requests routed to the existing catalogue
or a volunteer. *Causal Mechanism* then asks whether volunteer hints could explain
an apparent gain. The revised test alternates the two aids on comparable tasks,
uses a consistent assistance rule and records help separately, alongside time,
wrong turns and whether the first shelf is suitable. The ten-question probe can
expose failures; it cannot establish a reliable effect size. Drop the index if it
requires title-level upkeep or sends readers to unsuitable categories.

For **the existing-duty-slot pilot**, *Downside and Reversibility* challenges the
idea that a quiet rota slot is dispensable: it might provide someone's only
assisted access. Before scheduling, both volunteers would identify the
beneficiary and confirm equivalent access. The revised proposal is one
reversible session within existing duties, with the original duty immediately
restorable. If either volunteer disagrees or essential access cannot be
preserved, keep the rota unchanged. No added weekly hours are allowed;
feasibility and preparation time remain unknown.
